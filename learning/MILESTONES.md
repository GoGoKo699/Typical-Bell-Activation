# The 30-milestone understanding map

This map follows the scientific dependencies of Typical Bell Activation. Its thirty milestones connect the physical experiment, the inherited compatibility framework, the quantitative construction, and the meaning of the resulting noise threshold. Each milestone identifies an understanding target; detailed proofs and reproducible calculations remain in the linked scientific sources.

The educational anchor is Valerio Scarani, *Bell Nonlocality*, **first edition (2019)**. The core reading route uses [Chapter 2][Scarani-2], [Chapter 3][Scarani-3], and [Chapter 5][Scarani-5]. Optional notation refreshers come from the same book's [Appendix C][Scarani-C]. The [anchor and bridge](ANCHOR.md) explain the relevant passages and the additional derivations supplied by this project. The compatibility norm, quantitative Haar estimates, and universal noisy-POVM endpoint also have primary research sources; those references are distinct from the educational anchor.

## Allocation and scientific story

| Phase | Stable milestones | Role in the story |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Background/preliminaries](#background) | M01–M14: 14 milestones | Start with one qubit and a local measurement, then build joint measurements, composite Bell behaviors, probability estimates, and the complete quantified activation question. The project connects Bell geometry, compatibility, and random-state methods, so these foundations require a substantial part of the map. |
| [Narrative/innovation](#narrative) | M15–M30: 16 milestones | Start with the local dual certificate, assemble the multipartite Bell score and its bounds, then connect the theorem to an exact example, independent local noise, party counts, evidence costs, and the full scientific conclusion. |

The closing milestone of each phase integrates its preceding ideas. The map provides a route to the whole result without treating every technical lemma or adjacent topic as a separate milestone. The [walkthrough](walkthrough.md) follows these same IDs; the [learning overview](README.md) provides navigation.

## Phase I — Background/preliminaries

<a id="M01"></a>

### M01 — Single-qubit states and outcome probabilities

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What physical objects determine the probability of a recorded measurement outcome?
- **Understanding target:** Distinguish a qubit state, a measurement choice, and its possible outcome. Interpret $`\operatorname{Tr}(\rho M_{a\mid x})`$ as an outcome probability, with $`\rho`$ describing the state and the positive effects $`M_{a\mid x}`$ summing to the identity for each setting.
- **Dependencies and story role:** No earlier milestone is required. This establishes the local physical objects from which the entire experiment is composed.
- **Conceptual connection:** The later Bell expressions are functions of ordinary outcome probabilities generated by states and local measurements. Their auxiliary mathematical operators will not be extra physical measurements.
- **Source pointers:** [Scarani §3.1][Scarani-3]; optional [Appendix C, especially C.3.1][Scarani-C]; [physical model](../science/model-and-results.md#physical-model); [notation bridge](ANCHOR.md#notation).

<a id="M02"></a>

### M02 — Binary observables and local visibility

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** How can an experiment always record $`\pm1`$ while its observable has eigenvalues smaller than one in magnitude?
- **Understanding target:** Read $`M_{a\mid x}=(I+a\mathbf a_x\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/2`$ and distinguish binary outcome labels from the eigenvalues of the expectation operator $`A_x=\mathbf a_x\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma`$. Explain how shrinking the Bloch vector describes an unsharp binary measurement and how visibility $`\eta`$ changes it.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M01](#M01). It specifies the local measurement family before any multipartite construction is introduced.
- **Conceptual connection:** Measurement noise enters the theorem through the supplied Bloch vectors. The same visibility later describes projective measurements performed after local depolarization.
- **Source pointers:** [Scarani §3.1][Scarani-3]; optional [Appendix C.3][Scarani-C]; [measurement parametrization](../science/compatibility-dual.md#1-measurements-and-the-primal-norm); [moving noise to measurements](../science/local-noise-threshold.md#2-moving-the-noise-to-the-measurements).

<a id="M03"></a>

### M03 — Pauli coordinates, identity terms, and purity

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** Why are Pauli expectations useful coordinates for a qubit state?
- **Understanding target:** Recognize $`I,X,Y,Z`$ as a basis for Hermitian qubit operators, with the identity coefficient fixing trace and the three Pauli expectations giving a Bloch vector. Explain why purity is expressed by a quadratic sum of these coordinates, and why an identity term represents normalization rather than another physical direction.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M01](#M01) and [M02](#M02). It establishes the coordinate system that will later extend to Pauli words on many qubits.
- **Conceptual connection:** The project's quantum Bell score becomes a weighted sum of squared Pauli expectations. Keeping the identity coordinate explicit also fixes its constant term.
- **Source pointers:** Optional [Scarani Appendix C.1.2][Scarani-C]; [quantum realization context, §3.1][Scarani-3]; [Pauli-score expansion](../science/bell-construction.md#quantum-score-as-a-nonnegative-pauli-polynomial); [purity in the finite example](../science/finite-example.md#state-and-coefficients).

<a id="M04"></a>

### M04 — Joint measurements and response tables

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** When can several measurement choices be produced from one parent measurement?
- **Understanding target:** Explain joint measurability through a parent outcome followed by a classical response for each requested setting. Distinguish this common-parent condition from measuring observables sequentially, and recognize the role of complete sign strings $`s\in\{\pm1\}^m`$ in a parent description.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M02](#M02). It introduces the local compatibility property that the project will connect to global Bell correlations.
- **Conceptual connection:** A parent outcome supplies a classical response table. Compatible families at separated parties therefore provide the ingredients for a Bell-local model, while an incompatibility certificate identifies the resource used by the construction.
- **Source pointers:** [Scarani Appendix C.3.3][Scarani-C]; [exact equivalence with joint measurability](../science/compatibility-dual.md#2-exact-equivalence-with-joint-measurability); [compatible families in the Bell model](../science/model-and-results.md#compatible-families-and-the-converse-bound).

<a id="M05"></a>

### M05 — Compatibility as a norm and a dual certificate

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** How can a numerical certificate establish that a measurement family has no parent measurement?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret the compatibility norm $`\nu`$, its exact threshold $`\nu\le1`$, and a feasible dual tuple whose every signed sum has length at most one. Distinguish the optimal value $`\nu`$ from a feasible achieved value; an achieved objective greater than one already certifies incompatibility.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M03](#M03) and [M04](#M04). It completes the inherited local compatibility framework before the narrative uses its certificate in a new Bell construction.
- **Conceptual connection:** Convex duality turns the absence of a common measurement into concrete vectors $`\mathbf v_x`$. These vectors will constrain every classical response table and generate the Bell coefficients.
- **Source pointers:** [Primal norm](../science/compatibility-dual.md#1-measurements-and-the-primal-norm); [dual formula and attribution](../science/compatibility-dual.md#3-dual-formula-and-tensor-norm-attribution); primary compatibility sources [BJN2022](../references/README.md#bjn2022), [BN2022](../references/README.md#bn2022), and [Grinko–Uola2025](../references/README.md#grinko-uola2025); [steps beyond the anchor](ANCHOR.md#beyond-the-anchor).

<a id="M06"></a>

### M06 — Composing qubits into separated Bell parties

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What changes when the source distributes one qubit to each of $`N`$ separated parties?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret the total dimension $`D=2^N`$, tensor products of local measurement operators, and a potentially entangled shared state. Distinguish the locality of each measurement from a product-state assumption about the source, and extend single-qubit Pauli coordinates to Pauli words.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M01](#M01)–[M03](#M03). This moves the background from single-site objects to the global physical system.
- **Conceptual connection:** The shared state can correlate local outcomes even though the parties perform separate measurements. The proof will exploit the tensor structure of the measurement operators while drawing the state from the full $`N`$-qubit space.
- **Source pointers:** [Scarani §3.1][Scarani-3] and [Chapter 5][Scarani-5]; optional [Appendix C.1.3][Scarani-C]; [physical model](../science/model-and-results.md#physical-model); [Pauli words](../science/haar-moments.md#2-exact-pauli-fourth-moments).

<a id="M07"></a>

### M07 — Trial data, subset correlators, and marginalization

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** How can one Bell data set describe correlations of the whole system and of its subsets?
- **Understanding target:** Translate conditional outcome distributions into products of $`\pm1`$ outcomes and their averages. Explain $`x_i=0`$ as omission of a party from a correlator, not an extra setting or outcome-conditioned selection, and keep $`E_{\mathbf0}=1`$ explicit.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M03](#M03) and [M06](#M06). It connects the composite experiment to the complete data representation used by the theorem.
- **Conceptual connection:** Proper-subset correlators are ordinary marginals obtained from retained trials. Their inclusion is essential to the construction's scope and to its comparison with full-correlation results.
- **Source pointers:** [Scarani §§2.1–2.4][Scarani-2]; [marginal-correlator model](../science/model-and-results.md#marginal-correlators); [marginal bridge](ANCHOR.md#marginals); [measurement and description counts](../science/resources.md#measurement-and-description-counts).

<a id="M08"></a>

### M08 — Local behaviors as mixtures of deterministic strategies

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What is the full classical comparison class for this Bell experiment?
- **Understanding target:** Explain local conditional responses with shared randomness and their reduction to mixtures of deterministic assignments. Identify the $`2^{mN}`$ response arrays, and recognize that their signs need not obey quantum Bloch-vector constraints for incompatible settings.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M04](#M04), [M06](#M06), and [M07](#M07). It assembles local response tables into the global local polytope.
- **Conceptual connection:** The proof must control every classical response array. Restricting classical strategies to patterns realizable by a single qubit would give the wrong Bell bound.
- **Source pointers:** [Scarani §§2.1–2.4][Scarani-2]; [absolute local normalization](../science/model-and-results.md#absolute-local-normalization); [deterministic-score identity](../science/bell-construction.md#deterministic-scores-are-tensor-quadratic-forms).

<a id="M09"></a>

### M09 — Bell separation and absolute local normalization

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** How does a linear score separate observed correlations from all local behaviors?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret a Bell coefficient tensor, its quantum value, and $`L_{\mathrm{abs}}`$, the maximum absolute deterministic local score. Explain why checking deterministic vertices bounds all local mixtures, why coefficient rescaling preserves the ratio, and why changing the constant term requires a new local bound.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M07](#M07) and [M08](#M08). It converts the local polytope into the operational inequality the project will violate.
- **Conceptual connection:** The absolute convention controls positive and negative classical scores and is later required by the signed-flip converse. The constructed constant $`c_{\mathbf0}=1`$ remains in both parts of the ratio.
- **Source pointers:** [Scarani §§2.1–2.4][Scarani-2]; [normalization definition](../science/model-and-results.md#absolute-local-normalization); [absolute-bound bridge](ANCHOR.md#absolute-bound); [worked explanation](walkthrough.md).

<a id="M10"></a>

### M10 — Ordinary and genuine multipartite nonlocality

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** Which classical models are excluded by violating the bound used in this project?
- **Understanding target:** Distinguish failure of a fully local model from stronger notions of genuine multipartite nonlocality. Explain why the partition into separate one-qubit parties matters and why neither entanglement alone nor an ordinary Bell violation settles every stronger multipartite claim.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M06](#M06), [M08](#M08), and [M09](#M09). It fixes the global physical meaning of the eventual conclusion.
- **Conceptual connection:** The theorem excludes the fully local set. Its noise endpoint concerns measurements on one qubit per party; regrouping qubits and allowing collective local operations changes the experiment.
- **Source pointers:** [Scarani §5.1.1 and §§5.3.1–5.3.2][Scarani-5]; [multipartite-scope bridge](ANCHOR.md#multipartite-scope); [scope of the result](../science/model-and-results.md#scope-and-further-consequences); [meaning of the threshold](../science/resources.md#meaning-of-the-threshold).

<a id="M11"></a>

### M11 — Haar-random states and the meaning of typicality

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What distribution over pure states is meant by a typical quantum state here?
- **Understanding target:** Explain unitary-invariant Haar sampling and the normalized complex-Gaussian construction. Distinguish a particular randomly chosen pure state from the maximally mixed average of the ensemble, and identify that the theorem's probability is over states rather than over measurement outcomes alone.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M03](#M03) and [M06](#M06). It introduces the global state ensemble whose behavior the project studies.
- **Conceptual connection:** The Bell coefficients are matched to each sampled state. Averaging the density matrix first would erase that correlation between the state and the coefficient prescription.
- **Source pointers:** [Scarani §3.1][Scarani-3] for quantum behaviors; locally supplied [pure-state moment identity](../science/haar-moments.md#1-pure-state-moment-identity) and [Gaussian representation](../science/haar-moments.md#6-hermitian-quadratic-forms-and-gaussian-normalization); [state-tailoring bridge](ANCHOR.md#state-tailoring).

<a id="M12"></a>

### M12 — Means, variances, and exceptional states

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** Why is a large average score insufficient to show that most states have a large score?
- **Understanding target:** Distinguish an expectation from a high-probability guarantee. Explain how a relative-variance bound controls a lower tail and how a union bound combines exceptional events without assuming independence.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M11](#M11). It supplies the probability reasoning needed to turn an average quantum advantage into a typical-state statement.
- **Conceptual connection:** The proof must establish quantum growth and classical control for the same state. Exact moments and explicit exceptional probabilities make that coexistence quantitative.
- **Source pointers:** Locally supplied [weighted variance lemma](../science/haar-moments.md#3-a-weighted-variance-lemma) and [uniform positive-score bound](../science/haar-moments.md#5-positive-quadratic-forms-have-an-exponential-tail); [steps beyond the anchor](ANCHOR.md#beyond-the-anchor).

<a id="M13"></a>

### M13 — Growth rates and finite-size bounds

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What does exponential amplification mean when a bound also contains finite-size prefactors and failure probabilities?
- **Understanding target:** Compare a factor such as $`g^N`$ with a polynomial prefactor, keeping $`m`$ and the measurement family fixed. Distinguish an asymptotic conclusion from a useful finite-$`N`$ certificate, and interpret a probability lower bound that is zero at small sizes.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M06](#M06), [M09](#M09), and [M12](#M12). It connects the multipartite size parameter to the quantitative language of the main theorem.
- **Conceptual connection:** Products of local factors produce the competing exponential scales; concentration and strategy counting contribute prefactors. A positive exponent does not identify the optimal first violating party count.
- **Source pointers:** [Main finite-party theorem](../science/model-and-results.md#main-finite-party-theorem); [finite-party sufficiency bound](../science/party-count.md#2-the-finite-party-sufficiency-bound); [scaling near compatibility](../science/party-count.md#3-scaling-close-to-joint-measurability).

<a id="M14"></a>

### M14 — The activation question and its quantifiers

- **Phase:** Background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What exactly is being held fixed, sampled, constructed, and proved as the number of parties grows?
- **Understanding target:** Assemble the statement: fix a finite incompatible unbiased binary qubit family; use it at every party; draw a Haar state; prescribe coefficients from that state; and ask for Bell ratios that grow with high probability as $`N`$ increases. Distinguish this from a single fixed inequality for all states and from qualitative existence of some activating state.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Integrates [M05](#M05) and [M07](#M07)–[M13](#M13). It closes the background phase by stating the complete scientific problem with its assumptions and conclusion types.
- **Conceptual connection:** Compatibility supplies the local resource, Bell geometry supplies the classical comparison, and Haar probability specifies the desired global prevalence. The narrative phase will connect these three structures constructively.
- **Source pointers:** [Model and results](../science/model-and-results.md); [what is fixed and what is chosen](../science/resources.md#what-is-fixed-and-what-is-chosen); [known qualitative activation, PGQ2025](../references/README.md#pgq2025); [Scarani Chapters 2][Scarani-2], [3][Scarani-3], and [5][Scarani-5] for the Bell framework.

## Phase II — Narrative/innovation

<a id="M15"></a>

### M15 — Aligning a local incompatibility certificate

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How can a compatibility witness also provide a positive matrix suitable for a Bell-score calculation?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the transfer matrix $`T=\sum_x\mathbf a_x\mathbf v_x^{\mathsf T}`$, why a common rotation of the dual vectors preserves feasibility, and how polar alignment gives $`0\preceq T\preceq I`$. Identify its eigenvalues $`\lambda_j\in[0,1]`$ and trace $`\nu`$ for an optimal certificate.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M05](#M05) and [M14](#M14). This begins the narrative with the one-site mechanism that will make the many-body quantum score nonnegative.
- **Conceptual connection:** A convex incompatibility certificate becomes a positive contraction in Pauli coordinates. Only the auxiliary vectors are aligned; the supplied physical measurements remain fixed.
- **Source pointers:** [Polar alignment and transfer matrix](../science/compatibility-dual.md#4-polar-alignment-and-the-transfer-matrix); [certificate hypotheses for the Bell construction](../science/bell-construction.md); [beyond the anchor](ANCHOR.md#beyond-the-anchor).

<a id="M16"></a>

### M16 — From a certificate to state-dependent Bell coefficients

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How does a fixed local certificate specify a Bell inequality for a chosen target state?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret the auxiliary operators $`B_x^{(\tau)}=\tau\mathbf v_x\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma`$ and coefficients $`c_{\mathbf x}^{(\tau)}=\langle\bigotimes_iB_{x_i}^{(\tau)}\rangle_\psi`$. Distinguish these coefficient-generating operators from the actual measurements, retain $`B_0=I`$ and $`c_{\mathbf0}=1`$, and keep the functional fixed before evaluating experimental evidence.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M03](#M03), [M07](#M07), [M09](#M09), and [M15](#M15). It turns the one-site certificate into a complete multipartite coefficient prescription.
- **Conceptual connection:** The state supplies the directions of a separating functional in correlation space. Choosing coefficients from a state description changes neither the local comparison class nor the physical measurement menu.
- **Source pointers:** [Coefficient construction and normalization](../science/bell-construction.md#the-coefficients-and-their-normalization); [state-tailoring bridge](ANCHOR.md#state-tailoring); [worked explanation](walkthrough.md); [what is fixed and what is chosen](../science/resources.md#what-is-fixed-and-what-is-chosen).

<a id="M17"></a>

### M17 — Deterministic strategies become tensor operators

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How can all classical response tables be evaluated using operators on the same state space?
- **Understanding target:** Derive the meaning of $`L_s^{(\tau)}(\psi)=\langle\psi|F_s^{(\tau)}|\psi\rangle`$ from distributivity, with $`F_s^{(\tau)}`$ a tensor product of local signed sums. Explain why these operators are fixed independently of the Haar state, have trace $`D`$, and exhaust the deterministic local scores.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M08](#M08) and [M16](#M16). It assembles local response signs into the global classical object that must be bounded.
- **Conceptual connection:** The classical polytope has many vertices, but every vertex is evaluated by a quadratic form from one structured operator family. For the positive scaling, dual feasibility also makes every local factor positive.
- **Source pointers:** [Deterministic-score identity](../science/bell-construction.md#deterministic-scores-are-tensor-quadratic-forms); [positive-dual bound](../science/bell-construction.md#simpler-positive-dual-bound); [Scarani §§2.1–2.4][Scarani-2] for the deterministic-vertex interpretation.

<a id="M18"></a>

### M18 — The quantum score becomes a weighted Pauli sum

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Why does the same coefficient prescription yield a nonnegative quadratic expression for the quantum score?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the local contraction $`C_\tau=\operatorname{diag}(1,\tau T)`$ and its diagonalization into weights $`1,\tau\lambda_1,\tau\lambda_2,\tau\lambda_3`$. Recognize $`Q_\tau=1+\sum_{P\ne I}w_P\langle P\rangle_\psi^2`$, with nonnegative product weights, and identify how their total weight factorizes across parties.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M03](#M03), [M06](#M06), [M15](#M15), and [M16](#M16). It builds the global quantum counterpart to the classical tensor operators.
- **Conceptual connection:** Positivity of the aligned one-site matrix removes cancellations from the quantum score. Its tensor powers turn a local incompatibility parameter into a many-party growth factor.
- **Source pointers:** [Quantum-score expansion](../science/bell-construction.md#quantum-score-as-a-nonnegative-pauli-polynomial); [product-weight families](../science/haar-moments.md#4-the-two-product-weight-families); [transfer-matrix eigenvalues](../science/compatibility-dual.md#4-polar-alignment-and-the-transfer-matrix).

<a id="M19"></a>

### M19 — Why the quantum growth is typical

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Why is the growing quantum mean realized by most states rather than by a few exceptional ones?
- **Understanding target:** Connect the total Pauli weight to the exact Haar mean $`\mathbb E Q_\tau=[D+(1+\tau\nu)^N]/(D+1)`$. Explain how fourth moments bound the relative variance of the nonconstant part and why this supplies a lower-tail guarantee for the actual score of a typical state.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M11](#M11), [M12](#M12), and [M18](#M18). It turns the quantum-side algebra into a probabilistic many-party statement.
- **Conceptual connection:** The mean identifies the potential amplification; the fourth-moment estimate prevents rare large values from carrying that mean. Both are needed before comparing quantum and classical scores on the same state.
- **Source pointers:** [Exact Pauli fourth moments](../science/haar-moments.md#2-exact-pauli-fourth-moments); [weighted variance lemma](../science/haar-moments.md#3-a-weighted-variance-lemma); [weight conditions](../science/haar-moments.md#4-the-two-product-weight-families); [exact quantum mean](../science/bell-construction.md#quantum-score-as-a-nonnegative-pauli-polynomial).

<a id="M20"></a>

### M20 — Uniform control over all classical strategies

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Why does maximizing over exponentially many deterministic strategies not erase the quantum growth?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the positive-operator tail bound for a fixed trace-$`D`$ operator and how a union bound over $`2^{mN}`$ assignments gives a simultaneous local bound of order $`mN`$. Distinguish controlling one strategy from controlling their maximum, without requiring independence between their scores.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M12](#M12), [M13](#M13), and [M17](#M17). It upgrades the classical side from individual tensor operators to the complete local polytope.
- **Conceptual connection:** An exponential tail pays for an exponential strategy count through a linear threshold. The local bound is a high-probability statement about the sampled state, rather than an unconditional numerical certificate for every possible state.
- **Source pointers:** [Positive quadratic-form tail](../science/haar-moments.md#5-positive-quadratic-forms-have-an-exponential-tail); [positive-dual classical bound](../science/bell-construction.md#simpler-positive-dual-bound); [interpretation of the analytical local-score estimate](../science/resources.md#measurement-and-description-counts).

<a id="M21"></a>

### M21 — Completing the positive-dual activation mechanism

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How do the quantum lower bound and uniform classical upper bound establish activation together?
- **Understanding target:** Combine the two good events for the same state, retain the constant in both scores, and explain the ratio $`R_N^{(1)}\ge[(1+\nu)/2]^N/(8t_N)`$. Identify why $`\nu>1`$ gives exponential growth and why compatible families remain local for every state.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Integrates [M14](#M14), [M19](#M19), and [M20](#M20). It completes the first full construction before the stronger scaling is introduced.
- **Conceptual connection:** The local certificate has now crossed all three levels: single-qubit compatibility, the global Bell functional, and Haar typicality. The positive proof exposes the mechanism using only nonnegative deterministic operators.
- **Source pointers:** [Positive-dual proof](../science/bell-construction.md#simpler-positive-dual-bound); [simpler theorem statement](../science/model-and-results.md#simpler-positive-dual-theorem); [all-state locality for compatible families](../science/model-and-results.md#compatible-families-and-the-converse-bound).

<a id="M22"></a>

### M22 — Improving amplification with a scaled dual

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How can rescaling the auxiliary certificate improve the ratio even though its classical operators lose positivity?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the choice $`\tau=\nu`$, the continued positivity of the quantum Pauli weights, and the role of Hilbert–Schmidt and operator norms in controlling indefinite classical score operators. Connect the quantum scale $`b^N`$ and local scale $`O_m(N)b^{N/2}`$, where $`b=(1+\nu^2)/2`$.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M15](#M15), [M17](#M17)–[M21](#M21). It strengthens the completed construction by changing only auxiliary coefficients.
- **Conceptual connection:** Scalar rescaling changes both sides of the Bell comparison. The best scalar choice in this estimate yields $`g(\nu)=\sqrt{(1+\nu^2)/2}`$; it does not optimize all states or Bell inequalities.
- **Source pointers:** [Scaled-dual proof and scalar comparison](../science/bell-construction.md#main-scaled-dual-bound); [scaled product weights](../science/haar-moments.md#4-the-two-product-weight-families); [Hermitian Gaussian tail and normalization](../science/haar-moments.md#6-hermitian-quadratic-forms-and-gaussian-normalization).

<a id="M23"></a>

### M23 — The main finite-probability theorem

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** What is the precise quantitative guarantee once the improved construction is assembled?
- **Understanding target:** Read the main finite-$`N`$ ratio and exceptional-probability bounds with their quantifiers. <a id="M24"></a>

### M24 — Checking an exact six-qubit witness

- **Phase:** Narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** What can be certified for one fully specified finite state, and what does that establish about the general theorem?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the independent ingredients of the supplied witness: the normalized integer-amplitude state, the Pauli-purity quantum score, exhaustive evaluation of all deterministic local responses, and the exact ratio $`3816/3601>1`$. Distinguish a certified instance from a claim about Haar frequencies, and a failed noisy witness from a proof of locality.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses [M09](#M09), [M16](#M16), and [M23](#M23). It connects the analytical construction to a complete finite object with an unconditional local bound.
- **Conceptual connection:** The finite example verifies the coefficient and normalization conventions directly. Its exact arithmetic complements the asymptotic proof without replacing the probability argument.

### M25 — Translating activation to independent local noise
